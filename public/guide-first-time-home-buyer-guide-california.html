First-Time Buyers

California First-Time Home Buyer Guide: What to Prepare Before Shopping

A straightforward checklist for California first-time buyers covering credit, cash, preapproval, inspections, closing costs and ownership reserves.

By Sal Gharibyar, Broker/Owner · DRE #01418692
Published August 2026 · Expanded practical guide

This article provides general educational information. Real estate, lending, tax and legal circumstances vary by property and person.

Review credit and financing early

A lender can explain available loan programs, estimated payment, down payment and documentation requirements. Buyers should compare total costs, not only the advertised interest rate.

Review credit, income, debts, savings and total cash to close before shopping. Keep funds documented and avoid unexplained transfers.

Build a complete cash plan

Plan for down payment, deposit, inspections, appraisal, closing costs, moving and an emergency reserve after closing.

A lender may approve more than feels comfortable. Include taxes, insurance, mortgage insurance, HOA dues, utilities and maintenance.

Choose needs versus preferences

Separate essential requirements—such as location, bedrooms and payment—from features that would be nice but are not necessary.

Compare conventional, FHA, VA and other programs by payment, cash required, mortgage insurance, property standards and long-term cost.

Do not skip inspections

Even a newer or attractive home can have defects. A general inspection may identify the need for roof, sewer, electrical, structural or other specialist reviews.

Rank location, commute, layout, condition and future plans so homes can be compared objectively.

Expect the process to move quickly

Competitive markets may require fast decisions, but buyers should still understand the contract, disclosures and risks before removing contingencies.

Understand deposit, inspection, financing and appraisal terms before modifying or removing any protection.
